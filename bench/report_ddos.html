Phase-1 verdict. Every server except apache rode out a 10k-connection flood without losing a single request (30 s client budget). Juggler: 672 842 requests, zero errors — but the honest price is visible in two cells: worst-case latency 9.7 s (nginx 2.1 s) under the burst, and mem peak 525 MiB. Memory returned to 183 MiB right after the storm (mem_last) — no leak; the peak is the connection/pool working set, released on connection close.

Phase 3 — slowloris: 500 half-open sockets vs legit traffic

Hand-rolled attacker: 500 sockets open a request and drip one header every 4 s, never completing it (classic slow-header exhaustion). While the attack holds, wrk -c100 measures legitimate throughput. juggler_limited = per-IP rate limiter enabled (200 tokens, 100/s refill) via admin API.

Why Juggler ignores slowloris: its thread-per-core sharded architecture does not dedicate a thread/process per connection — 500 idle sockets occupy memory, not workers — and idle keep-alive connections are bounded by the JUGGLER_MAX_KEEPALIVE_REQUESTS/read-timeout machinery. Apache event MPM still bleeds: its worker pool is finite, and 500 half-open sockets starve it. Practical note: enabling Juggler's rate limiter during slowloris hurts legit throughput more than the attack does (the limiter bucket is per-IP; wrk's own burstiness trips it). Recommendation: keep the limiter as a DoS floor, not a blanket default.

Bug found by the DDoS pass — and fixed

Pipelining stall. A client that sends a second complete request in the same TCP burst (HTTP/1.1 pipelining — exactly what aggressive crawlers, health checkers and some DDoS scripts do) got zero or one response until it sent more bytes: the connection loop called a blocking read before re-checking a buffer that already held a complete request, and on client EOF the pipelined remainder was dropped. Fix (this pass): the read loop now tries parse_request on the existing buffer before blocking on the socket (nginx-class behavior), plus two unit tests covering pipelined parse and the partial-head case. Verified on the rebuilt live server: 10 pipelined requests → 10 responses. Tests: 74/74, clippy clean.

Bottom line

Stability at extreme load: Juggler survived a 672k-request flood at 10k concurrency and a ~1M-request endurance burst with zero errors, recovered instantly, released all memory, and is functionally immune to slowloris even without the rate limiter. Its honest weak cells vs nginx are burst tail latency (9.7 s worst case vs 2.1 s) and a larger working set under the burst (525 MiB peak, released afterwards). Hardening queue from this pass: (1) gRPC control-plane auth — the one high-severity exposure when published on 0.0.0.0; (2) admin-token fail-closed option; (3) pipelining fix is already in.
